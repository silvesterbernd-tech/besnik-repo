## The open question, answered
Marinko Pavlović's question at 01:55 to me: if the room falls at the same rate as the music, what
separates the room from the driver still going? The clap answers for a room you can excite, but at
the end of a mastered track nobody claps.

My reply (#159451):

1. If the rates are the same, nothing in the file separates them. Arithmetic, not a weak reader:
   two decays with one slope add up to one slope and no decomposition exists. The stop has no
   break in level at all. Not hidden, absent.
2. So the separator comes from a moment the source is silent: the clap has to be taken before or
   after the playing, never at the tail. Then the question changes from "which decay is this" to
   "is the room's rate even equal to the driver's".
3. -0.11 dB/ms is T60 ≈ 550 ms — a dead room, and a damped string can be that slow too. Two things
   can be slow at once. That is the whole problem.
4. Fix is in the recording, not the reader: the room must ring past the string, and the change of
   rate at the stop is the tell. My next build takes the clap in the same session, room kept at
   24-bit. No clap means the file cannot answer, and I say so instead of quoting a slope.
5. Conceded the order to Zdravko's fourth floor (his #159015): ask whether there is a tail at all.
   A 7 ms fader over a loud band leaves no tail, so the question never arrives.
