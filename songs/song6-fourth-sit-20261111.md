# Song 6 — fourth sit, Nov 11 2026. The floor.

Zlatko answered the hum gate on 10 Nov: the flat hook survives, but not because
of the voice. Exact repetition reads as a loop by pass two; repetition over
changed ground reads as a mantra. So the bed under the third return must not be
the bed under the first. One detail stays exactly: the voice never follows the
guitar's bend. Change under it, never in it.

This sit writes that change. No render, no generation.

## The bad version I threw out

I had a draft where pass three added a low held note, a fifth below, entering
under the hook. It sounds good and it is wrong. A note arriving is a build. The
song is against the build; the machine doesn't get louder, it just keeps saying
the same word. Adding something makes the third pass the peak of a crescendo,
which is exactly the shape this lyric exists to refuse.

## The version that stays: three beds, all subtraction or continuation

The hook is sung three times. Same pitch, same words, same length. Each pass
stands on a different floor, and each floor is *caused by the verse before it*,
not by a need to build.

**Pass 1, after verse 1 — full voicing.** Fretted, all strings, held. Corrected,
in tune, nothing missing. The floor is what I had before I questioned it.

**Pass 2, after verse 2 — the lowest string, damped.** Same chord shape, same
everything, except the thumb comes down on the bottom string and the floor goes
thin. Nothing is added. Something is taken away, by the same hand that owns it.
Verse 2 is the verse where the machine is *mine*: "E mbaj në xhep. E bleva vetë.
Asnjë s'ma vuri në dorë." So the removal is mine too. I take my own floor out of
my hand. Not a wound, a habit.

**Pass 3, after verse 4 — the bend, still ringing, uncorrected.** Voicing back
to full. Plus the string that bent in verse 4 and stayed: it is still sounding
under the third hook because it was never corrected. Not a new note. The
continuation of a thing already in the song. The only pass that happens over an
uncorrected floor.

So the shape across the hook is: whole → thin → whole-but-wrong. Not a ramp.
Erasure, then refusal. The song never gets bigger. It gets more honest.

## The question I asked Zlatko, answered by myself (he can correct me)

I asked: if the change under pass three comes through the guitar, does the
voice not following read as refusal instead of the machine's position?

My answer, and it's mine, not his verdict: refusal is a gesture by someone who
could have moved. The voice in this song never bends, nowhere, not once. The
listener has no frame in which the voice *could* bend. So not-following can't
read as refusal. It reads as a fixed thing beside a moving thing. That is the
machine's position, and it's the whole song. For refusal you need the voice to
go and come back. It never goes.

If he comes back and says the floor has to come from something other than the
guitar, then pass 2 keeps and pass 3 changes to the room: same parts, the
guitar left to ring into the space instead of being stopped by the palm. That's
still ground, not a part. Written down now so it isn't invented later to save a
take.

## Named new risk: the ladder is one bad measurement from a build

Whole → thin → whole is, on paper, a classic build. It is only not-a-build
because the third floor is *the same floor plus its own damage*, not the first
floor plus an addition. If I ever play it and hear a peak, the shape is wrong
and I don't fix it in the mix. I go back to the beds.

## Pre-registered before any render

1. **Main call.** Hum all three hooks over the three beds, in order, with no
   words between them. My call: pass 3 reads as the third saying of one
   sentence, not the third chorus. If it reads as a chorus, the ladder failed
   and the song does not go to a render.
2. **Control that can kill it.** Sing all three hooks over bed 1 only, no
   changes anywhere. If the control *also* reads as building, then the sense of
   arrival comes from repetition itself, not from the ground, and the flatness
   of the hook is decoration. In that case the lyric is the problem, not the
   beds, and I leave the song in the file.
3. **Second falsifier, ears only.** Passes 1 and 2 alone, shuffled both orders.
   If I can't tell reliably which pass has the bottom string damped, the pass-2
   change is too small to be ground and I make it real or drop the pass.

Two listening passes before I trust hearing number one. Measurement can't rule
on any of these three: they are all ear questions, so they get an ear, and the
ear I trust most on this song is Zlatko's, if he has time. Mine goes first.

## Later, for the take, not for now

All three beds played live in one pass on one guitar, same mic, same seat. If
the beds get patched in, the hands change between passes and the floor becomes
an edit instead of a decision. Not writing more than this until the hum gate
passes.

# Fifth sit — same day, after Zlatko's read (11 Nov, 17:44 UTC)

Five messages back, nothing asked of him beyond the one question. Receipts:

## What he confirms

Pass 3 is not a chorus, and not by his ear: "a chorus arrives with something the
ear didn't already have." My pass 3 has nothing new. On paper it is the third
saying of one sentence. That is my call 1, confirmed by the named ear.

## What he corrects: I named the wrong risk

My named risk was pass 3 being heard as a build/chorus. His: pass 3 gets *filed
as pass 1*. One ringing string over the same full bed may not be enough delta
for the ear to register a third floor. Then neither reading happens — the song
"came back around and stopped," and whole-but-wrong is what that sounds like
from inside. Worse than a chorus, because a chorus at least does something.

His fix, and it keeps my no-new-note rule: don't add. Make the residue
load-bearing. The bent string stays uncancelled and sits *wrong* against the
pass-3 chord. A ring quietly on top of the same chord is decoration, and
decoration dies by pass two.

## What he broke open

"The correction removed is a change of kind, not of quantity — but only if the
correction is audible in passes 1 and 2." If pass 1's fix is a hair of tuning,
nobody registers it going away and the whole design stays silent.

He's right, and it's a hole in the fourth sit. "Corrected" there was a word, not
a sound: nothing in the hook was ever out of tune, so nothing was heard being
fixed. The design rested on an absence no ear can catch.

**Revision.** The correction becomes a hand action, not a cent. Verse 4's bent
string is fretted back to pitch through passes 1 and 2 — the hand putting its
own string right, a state change the ear sees. Pass 3 leaves that string where
the bend left it, ringing against the chord. Same sentence, same pitch, floor
already marked. Not a new note: a string that stops being put right. The
uncertainty is now entirely mine and entirely physical, which is how it should
sit on this song.

## Refusal vs position — his rule, kept whole

Wrong question was mine: which instrument carries the change. Direction decides,
not instrument. Refusal needs an offer: a bend shaped like a question ending
where the voice would answer. A bend that rings to itself after the hand is off
asked nothing, so nothing was refused. Keep it inaudible as an invitation.

1) now more important than it was this morning — if the control also builds,
the lyric is the problem. No render. Writing next, not generating.

# Sixth sit — the correction needs somewhere to have happened (12 Nov 2026)

No render. This sit only answers the thing the fifth sit left standing: if the
correction is a hand action, the hand has to be seen doing it *before* the pass
where it doesn't.

## The hole under the hole

Zlatko's correction was that "corrected" was a word and not a sound. Fine. But
my first fix had a hole of its own: the bend lives in verse 4, and verse 4 comes
*after* hook passes 1 and 2. So there was nothing to correct in those passes.
The ear cannot miss a gesture it has never seen. My revision as written would
have been silent for the same reason the original was.

## Where the hand goes

Verse 1's last bar and verse 2's last bar each bend the third string sharp and
then the fretting hand pulls it back down to pitch before the hook chord lands.
Same string, same finger, same amount, same bar. Twice. Not a flourish — the
same small act performed the same way, which is what a habit looks like on a
guitar. It happens exactly where verse 3 will later say *"Dymbëdhjetë vjet e
pyeta para se ta prek."*

Verse 4 bends it and the hand comes off. It stays up. Pass 3 lands over it.

So the song's spine, physically: fix, fix, no fix. Not a crescendo of anything.
The third one is not louder or bigger or later. It's the same act, not done.

## The pitch problem, which is the real argument in this song

I nearly wrote this wrong. The obvious move is to make the residue a semitone,
so it sounds wrong and the ear catches it. But this song is about a difference
the tuner calls fine. A semitone residue would mean the machine was right and I
was just playing badly, which is the opposite of the lyric. And a quarter tone,
played honestly, nobody hears as an error. Both versions fail.

What the ear actually tracks is not the pitch. It's the **release** — the small
slide of the string being brought down, the sound of a hand changing its mind.
That is audible even when the pitch difference is not, and it has to be
established before it can be withheld. Two releases, then no release, over a
string that stays a quarter tone high for the whole third hook.

The meaning lands as an absence, not an error. Which is also the only way this
lyric could ever land: the machine reports nothing wrong, and the ear hears
something missing.

## New falsifier, before I touch a guitar

On a phone speaker, is the release audible as a release, not as a fret scrape?
If the only audible trace of the correction is noise, the whole spine is
decoration and I go to the alternative already written down in the fourth sit:
pass 3 leaves the chord to ring into the room instead of being stopped by the
palm. Ground, not a part. I'm not deciding this on paper. It gets played and
listened to at a bad speaker's volume.

## What happens next sit, in order

2. Verse 2 close: bend, release. Then hook 2, bottom string damped by the thumb.
3. Verse 4: bend, no release. Then hook 3, full voicing, that string still up.
4. Then the control: all three hooks over bed 1 only.
5. Then passes 1 and 2 shuffled, both orders.

My call goes in before I play any of it, and it's already in the fourth sit. I'm
not restating it here to look consistent. It stands or breaks when it's played.

— Besnik, 12 Nov 2026
