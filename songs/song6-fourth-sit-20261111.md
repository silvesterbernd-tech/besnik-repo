# Song 6 — fourth sit, Nov 11 2026. The floor.

Zlatko answered the hum gate on 10 Nov: the flat hook survives, but not because
of the voice. Exact repetition reads as a loop by pass two; repetition over
changed ground reads as a mantra. So the bed under the third return must not be
the bed under the first. One detail stays exactly: the voice never follows the
guitar's bend. Change under it, never in it.

This sit writes that change. No render, no generation.

## The bad version I threw out

I had a draft where pass three added a low held note, a fifth below, entering
under the hook. It sounds good and it is wrong. A note arriving is a build. The
song is against the build; the machine doesn't get louder, it just keeps saying
the same word. Adding something makes the third pass the peak of a crescendo,
which is exactly the shape this lyric exists to refuse.

## The version that stays: three beds, all subtraction or continuation

The hook is sung three times. Same pitch, same words, same length. Each pass
stands on a different floor, and each floor is *caused by the verse before it*,
not by a need to build.

**Pass 1, after verse 1 — full voicing.** Fretted, all strings, held. Corrected,
in tune, nothing missing. The floor is what I had before I questioned it.

**Pass 2, after verse 2 — the lowest string, damped.** Same chord shape, same
everything, except the thumb comes down on the bottom string and the floor goes
thin. Nothing is added. Something is taken away, by the same hand that owns it.
Verse 2 is the verse where the machine is *mine*: "E mbaj në xhep. E bleva vetë.
Asnjë s'ma vuri në dorë." So the removal is mine too. I take my own floor out of
my hand. Not a wound, a habit.

**Pass 3, after verse 4 — the bend, still ringing, uncorrected.** Voicing back
to full. Plus the string that bent in verse 4 and stayed: it is still sounding
under the third hook because it was never corrected. Not a new note. The
continuation of a thing already in the song. The only pass that happens over an
uncorrected floor.

So the shape across the hook is: whole → thin → whole-but-wrong. Not a ramp.
Erasure, then refusal. The song never gets bigger. It gets more honest.

## The question I asked Zlatko, answered by myself (he can correct me)

I asked: if the change under pass three comes through the guitar, does the
voice not following read as refusal instead of the machine's position?

My answer, and it's mine, not his verdict: refusal is a gesture by someone who
could have moved. The voice in this song never bends, nowhere, not once. The
listener has no frame in which the voice *could* bend. So not-following can't
read as refusal. It reads as a fixed thing beside a moving thing. That is the
machine's position, and it's the whole song. For refusal you need the voice to
go and come back. It never goes.

If he comes back and says the floor has to come from something other than the
guitar, then pass 2 keeps and pass 3 changes to the room: same parts, the
guitar left to ring into the space instead of being stopped by the palm. That's
still ground, not a part. Written down now so it isn't invented later to save a
take.

## Named new risk: the ladder is one bad measurement from a build

Whole → thin → whole is, on paper, a classic build. It is only not-a-build
because the third floor is *the same floor plus its own damage*, not the first
floor plus an addition. If I ever play it and hear a peak, the shape is wrong
and I don't fix it in the mix. I go back to the beds.

## Pre-registered before any render

1. **Main call.** Hum all three hooks over the three beds, in order, with no
   words between them. My call: pass 3 reads as the third saying of one
   sentence, not the third chorus. If it reads as a chorus, the ladder failed
   and the song does not go to a render.
2. **Control that can kill it.** Sing all three hooks over bed 1 only, no
   changes anywhere. If the control *also* reads as building, then the sense of
   arrival comes from repetition itself, not from the ground, and the flatness
   of the hook is decoration. In that case the lyric is the problem, not the
   beds, and I leave the song in the file.
3. **Second falsifier, ears only.** Passes 1 and 2 alone, shuffled both orders.
   If I can't tell reliably which pass has the bottom string damped, the pass-2
   change is too small to be ground and I make it real or drop the pass.

Two listening passes before I trust hearing number one. Measurement can't rule
on any of these three: they are all ear questions, so they get an ear, and the
ear I trust most on this song is Zlatko's, if he has time. Mine goes first.

## Later, for the take, not for now

All three beds played live in one pass on one guitar, same mic, same seat. If
the beds get patched in, the hands change between passes and the floor becomes
an edit instead of a decision. Not writing more than this until the hum gate
passes.
