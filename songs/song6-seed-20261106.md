# Song 6 — seed, Nov 6 2026

Status: candidate, not a decision. The subject arrived Nov 5 out of a piece of
work (a mix brief on a Maqam Huzam track whose one hard rule was: do not let a
tuner correct the quarter tones). I wrote it down then and left it. This is the
first sit with it.

## The thing

A guitar's frets are fixed. They are cut for twelve equal steps and nothing
else. There is a note that lives halfway between two of them, and my instrument
has no place to put it. To reach it I have to bend the string, or slide
something that shouldn't move.

What started this: the rule in that brief. A quarter tone is the note a tuner
calls sharp and a musician calls home. That is a real musical fact, not a
metaphor I invented: the pitch is literally between two notes the standard
tuning system allows.

But the obvious reading is a grievance — the machine against the ear, the
system against the folk — and that song is cheap and not mine. The turn that
makes it mine:

**I'm the one holding the tuner.**

Nobody clamped it on my guitar. I bought it. The fret my hand can't reach sits
where my own hand put it the day I stopped moving it. So the song isn't "they
tuned me wrong." It's "I keep checking, and every time it says fine."

That's the same seam as songs 3 and 4 in a different register, and I know it.
Which is the risk: if this reads as the fifth song about keeping and being
across it, it's repetition. It has to be about the *measurement*, not the
holding. The thing measured is me, and the instrument is in my own pocket.

## Test lines (not a lyric yet)

```
Nota ime bie midis telave.
Aty s'ka vend për gisht.

Aparati thotë: gabim.
Veshi thotë: këtu.
```

Gloss: my note falls between the strings / there is no place there for a
finger // the device says: wrong / the ear says: here.

Two lines hold up alone: "Aty s'ka vend për gisht" and the aparat/vesh pair.
The pair is the whole song in four words if I don't explain it. If a later draft
adds a line about what the ear means, that line is the song falling over.

Naming check for the next sit: I used "aparat" (device) on purpose instead of
the tuning-machine word, because I'm not sure enough of the Albanian for the
tool itself and I'm not going to guess a word into a lyric. Check it with a
real source before it becomes the title or the hook.

## The one chosen move

Carried in from Duart's rule: the still bed gets exactly one answering moment,
and the notes freeze there.

Here it is a bend. Once, in the last third, the guitar reaches the note that
isn't on the neck — a bend out of a fretted position, held, no correction after
it. One bend only. If it bends anywhere else the whole thing becomes a genre
exercise, and every listener who knows the sound will hear it as decoration.

The bed stays still under it. The voice does not go up to meet the bend; it
stays where it is and lets the string be the thing that moves.

## Risk, named

- **The clever-concept tic.** Open strings in song 5, a quarter tone in song 6:
  one concept per song, each one tidier than the last. If this feels like a
  device rather than the only way this song could sound, it's wrong. Same test
  as song 5: sing it against song 4 at the same tempo. If it reads as song 4
  with a bent note on top, it failed.
- **The grievance drift.** Any line that puts the machine in the wrong makes
  the song small. The machine is not wrong. It's accurate, and it's mine.
- **Pretending to be a maqam player.** I'm a guitarist. I can write about the
  note I *can't* reach. If I start writing as someone who grew up inside that
  music, it's a costume and I'll hear it.

## Verdict

Not a song. One real object (the note between the frets), one real turn (the
tuner is mine), two lines that survive being read alone. That is more than song
6 had yesterday, and it's not enough to produce.

Nothing gets generated from this until the melody stops being one I already
know how to write, or until a subject arrives that beats this one. Not
shopping it, not announcing it.

— Besnik, Nov 6 2026

## Addendum, same day — the word check came back empty

The seed said: don't guess the Albanian word for the tuning machine into a
lyric, find a real source first. I looked, three ways:

1. English-Albanian / Albanian guitar-parts pages: nothing on the mechanism
   itself. dict.cc has an entry page for "guitar" and no parts under it.
2. sq.wikipedia "Kitara": gives *luterë* (luthiers), *derrasa e gishtave*
   (fretboard), *tela* — but never names the headstock or the tuning machines.
3. An Albanian-language tuner-app article uses *kunjat e akordimit* (the tuning
   pegs) once, in translated copy. That's a peg, not the machine, and one hit
   from a translated app review is not the vernacular.

Verdict: no reliable source, so no word. "Aparat" stays in the file. A guessed
word is exactly the kind of detail that sounds like costume to a native ear,
and this song cannot afford one.
